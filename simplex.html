The (Revised) Simplex Algorithm: the Gist

Author

Quentin Bertrand

What is the Simplex For?

The simplex algorithm solves linear problems in their standard form, i.e., the following type of optimization problems: \[\begin{align} \min_{x \in \mathbb{R}^n} \; & c^\top x \\ \text{s.t.} \; & A x = b \tag{Primal Problem}\label{eq_primal} \\ & x \geq 0 \enspace, \end{align}\] where \(c \in \mathbb{R}^n\), \(b \in \mathbb{R}^m\), and \(A \in \mathbb{R}^{m \times n}\).

The Optimality Conditions

The Karusk-Kuhn-Tucker optimality conditions write, for \(x \in \mathbb{R}^n, \lambda \in \mathbb{R}^m, \text{and } \mu \in \mathbb{R}^n\): \[\begin{align} & A^\top \lambda + \mu = c \tag{KKT-1}\label{eq-KKT1} \\ & Ax = b \\ & x \geq 0 \\ & \mu \geq 0 \\ & x_i \mu_i = 0 , \tag{Complementary Slackness}\label{eq-complslackness} \enspace \text{for } i \in 1, \dots, n \enspace . \end{align}\] Since the problem is convex, with linear equality and inequality constraints, the Karusk-Kuhn-Tucker conditions are necessary and sufficient for being a solution of the linear \(\ref{eq_primal}\). In other words, if one can find a triplet \((x, \lambda, \mu)\) that satisfies the KKT conditions, then \(x\) is an optimal point for the \(\ref{eq_primal}\).

Outline of Simplex Algorithm

The simplex algorithm is an active set algorithm: this is an iterative algorithm which starts from a (basic) feasible point \(x_0\) (see Definition 1), with only \(m\) non zero coefficients, and then successivley selects zero entries of \(x\) and make them non zero. In this section we present the notion of basic feasible point, the index selection rule, and the update rule.

Geometry of the Feasible Set

The simplex algorithm is an iterative algorithm which requires the initial point to be a basic feasible point. We will see later how to find such a point.

Definition 1 (Basic Feasible Point)

\(x\) is a basic feasible point if there exists a basis \(\mathcal{B}\) such that

  • \(|\mathcal{B}| = m\)
  • \(x_{\mathcal{B}^c} = 0\), i.e., \(x_i = 0\) if \(i \notin \mathcal{B}\)
  • \(A_{:\mathcal{B}}\) is nonsingular, i.e., the square matrix \(A_{:\mathcal{B}}\) is full rank

Index Selection

Let’s start from a basic feasible point \(x \in \mathbb{R}^n\), and let’s try to find a triplet \((x, \lambda, \mu)\) that satisifies the KKT conditions.

  • \(x\) is a (non generated) basic feasible point, hence \(x_i \neq 0\) for \(i \in \mathcal{B}\), thus Equation \(\ref{eq-complslackness}\) yields \[\begin{align} \mu_i = 0 \text{ for } i \in \mathcal{B} \enspace. \end{align}\]
  • The latest equation combined with the Equation \(\ref{eq-KKT1}\) yields \[\begin{align} & A_{:\mathcal{B}}^\top \lambda + \overbrace{\mu_{\mathcal{B}}}^{=0} = c_{\mathcal{B}} \\ & \lambda = (A_{:\mathcal{B}}^\top)^{-1} c_{\mathcal{B}} \enspace . \end{align}\]
  • Plugging the value of \(\lambda\) in Equation \(\ref{eq-KKT1}\) restricted to \(\mathcal{B}^c\) yields \[\begin{align} \mu_{\mathcal{B}^c} = c_{\mathcal{B}^c} - A_{\mathcal{B}^c}^\top (A_{:\mathcal{B}}^\top)^{-1} c_{\mathcal{B}} \enspace . \end{align}\]

Let’s recap the first part of this outline: we started from a (non degenerated) basic feasible point \(x\): the complementary slackness yields \(\mu_{\mathcal{B}}=0\), which yields a unique dual variable \(\lambda\), which yields a unique slack variable \(\mu_{\mathcal{B}^c}\) using the KKT conditions.

  • If in addition \(\mu\) satisfies \(\mu \geq 0\), then then triplet \((x, \lambda, \mu)\) satisfies all the KKT conditions. We can stop here!
  • If not, let’s pick an index \(q \in \mathcal{B}^c\) such that \(\mu_{q} < 0\), and make \(x_q\) non zero, e.g., \[\begin{align} q & \in \arg \min_{j \in \mathcal{B}^c} \, \mu_{j} \\ & = \arg \min_{j \in \mathcal{B}^c} c_{j} - A_{: j}^\top (A_{:\mathcal{B}}^\top)^{-1} c_{\mathcal{B}} \enspace . \end{align}\]
  • Question: how to updtae the entry \(x_q\)? Such that the new prinmal point \(x\) statifies the equality constraint \(Ax = b\) and the positivity constraint \(x \geq 0\)?

Update Rule

In other words, one wants to update the update coefficient of \(x^t\), such that \(x^{t+1}\) satisfies \(A x^{t+1} =b\), i.e., \[\begin{align} \underbrace{A x^t}_{= A_{:\mathcal{B}} x_{\mathcal{B}}^t} = b = \underbrace{A x^{t+1}}_{ = A_{:\mathcal{B}} x_{\mathcal{B}}^{t+1} + A_{:q} x_q^{t+1}} \enspace, \end{align}\] which yields \[\begin{align} x_{\mathcal{B}}^{t+1} = x_{\mathcal{B}}^{t} - \underbrace{x_q^{t+1}}_{\in \mathbb{R}} \cdot \overbrace{\underbrace{A_{:\mathcal{B}}^{-1} A_{:q}}_{:=d}}^{\text{requires solving a linear system}} \enspace. \end{align}\]

Question: how to chose \(x_q^{t+1}\) then? Simply by chosing \(x_q^{t+1}\) it as large possible, but still satisfy the constraint \(x^{t+1} \geq 0\):

\[\begin{align} x_q^{t+1} = \min_{i \in \mathcal{B} | d_i > 0} \frac{x_i}{d_i} \enspace, \end{align}\]

with \(d:= A_{:\mathcal{B}}^{-1} A_{:q}\) (\(d\) stands for direction of the update).

\begin{algorithm} \caption{(Revised) Simplex} \begin{algorithmic} \Procedure{Simplex}{$A, c, x_0$} \For{$i \in 0, \dots, n_{\mathrm{iter}} $} \State $\lambda = (A_{:\mathcal{B}}^\top)^{-1} c_{\mathcal{B}}$ \State $\mu_{\mathcal{B}^c} = c_{\mathcal{B}^c} - A_{\mathcal{B}^c}^\top (A_{:\mathcal{B}}^\top)^{-1} c_{\mathcal{B}} $ \IF{$\mu_j \geq 0$} \State Return $x^{t}$ \ENDIF \State $q \in \arg \min_{j \in \mathcal{B}^c} \, \mu_{j} $ \State $d = A_{:\mathcal{B}}^{-1} A_{:q}$ \State $ x_q^{t+1} = \min_{i \in \mathcal{B} | d_i > 0} \frac{x_i}{d_i} $ \State $x_{\mathcal{B}}^{t+1} = x_{\mathcal{B}}^{t} - x_q^{t+1} \cdot d$ \EndFor \Return x \EndProcedure \end{algorithmic} \end{algorithm}

## The Optimality Conditions

The Karusk-Kuhn-Tucker optimality conditions write, for
$x \in \mathbb{R}^n, \lambda \in \mathbb{R}^m, \text{and } \mu \in \mathbb{R}^n$:
\begin{align}
    & A^\top \lambda + \mu = c \tag{KKT-1}\label{eq-KKT1}
    \\
    & Ax = b
    \\
    & x \geq 0
    \\
    & \mu \geq 0
    \\
    & x_i \mu_i = 0 , \tag{Complementary Slackness}\label{eq-complslackness}
    \enspace \text{for } i \in 1, \dots, n
    \enspace .
\end{align}
Since the problem is convex, with linear equality and inequality constraints, the Karusk-Kuhn-Tucker conditions are necessary and sufficient for being a solution of the linear \ref{eq_primal}.
In other words, if one can find a triplet $(x, \lambda, \mu)$ that satisfies the KKT conditions, then $x$ is an optimal point for the \ref{eq_primal}.

## Outline of Simplex Algorithm

The simplex algorithm is an *active set algorithm*: this is an iterative algorithm which starts from a *(basic) feasible point* $x_0$ (see @def-feasibleset), with only $m$ non zero coefficients, and then successivley selects zero entries of $x$ and make them non zero.
In this section we present the notion of basic feasible point, the index selection rule, and the update rule.

### Geometry of the Feasible Set

The simplex algorithm is an iterative algorithm which requires the initial point to be a *basic feasible point*.
We will see later how to find such a point.

::: {#def-feasibleset}
#### Basic Feasible Point

<br>

$x$ is a *basic feasible point* if there exists a *basis* $\mathcal{B}$ such that

-  $|\mathcal{B}| = m$
-  $x_{\mathcal{B}^c} = 0$, *i.e.*,  $x_i = 0$ if $i \notin \mathcal{B}$
-  $A_{:\mathcal{B}}$ is nonsingular, *i.e.*, the square matrix $A_{:\mathcal{B}}$ is full rank
:::

### Index Selection

Let's start from a basic feasible point $x \in \mathbb{R}^n$, and let's try to find a triplet $(x, \lambda, \mu)$ that satisifies the KKT conditions.

- $x$ is a (non generated) basic feasible point, hence $x_i \neq 0$ for $i \in \mathcal{B}$, thus Equation \ref{eq-complslackness} yields
    \begin{align}
        \mu_i = 0 \text{ for } i \in \mathcal{B}
        \enspace.
    \end{align}
- The latest equation combined with the Equation \ref{eq-KKT1} yields
\begin{align}
    & A_{:\mathcal{B}}^\top \lambda + \overbrace{\mu_{\mathcal{B}}}^{=0} = c_{\mathcal{B}}
    \\
    & \lambda = (A_{:\mathcal{B}}^\top)^{-1} c_{\mathcal{B}}
    \enspace .
\end{align}
- Plugging the value of $\lambda$ in Equation \ref{eq-KKT1} restricted to $\mathcal{B}^c$ yields
\begin{align}
    \mu_{\mathcal{B}^c} = c_{\mathcal{B}^c} - A_{\mathcal{B}^c}^\top (A_{:\mathcal{B}}^\top)^{-1} c_{\mathcal{B}}
    \enspace .
\end{align}

Let's recap the first part of this outline: we started from a (non degenerated) basic feasible point $x$: the complementary slackness yields $\mu_{\mathcal{B}}=0$, which yields a unique dual variable $\lambda$, which yields a unique slack variable $\mu_{\mathcal{B}^c}$ using the KKT conditions.

- If in addition $\mu$ satisfies $\mu \geq 0$, then then triplet $(x, \lambda, \mu)$ satisfies all the KKT conditions. We can stop here!
- If not, let's pick an index $q \in \mathcal{B}^c$ such that $\mu_{q} < 0$, and make $x_q$ non zero, e.g.,
  \begin{align}
    q & \in \arg \min_{j \in \mathcal{B}^c}  \, \mu_{j} \\
    & = \arg \min_{j \in \mathcal{B}^c} c_{j} - A_{: j}^\top (A_{:\mathcal{B}}^\top)^{-1} c_{\mathcal{B}}
    \enspace .
\end{align}
- Question: how to updtae the entry $x_q$? Such that the new prinmal point $x$ statifies the equality constraint $Ax = b$ and the positivity constraint $x \geq 0$?

### Update Rule

In other words, one wants to update the update coefficient of $x^t$, such that $x^{t+1}$ satisfies $A x^{t+1} =b$, i.e.,
\begin{align}
    \underbrace{A x^t}_{= A_{:\mathcal{B}} x_{\mathcal{B}}^t}
    =
    b
    =
    \underbrace{A x^{t+1}}_{
        = A_{:\mathcal{B}} x_{\mathcal{B}}^{t+1}
        + A_{:q} x_q^{t+1}}
        \enspace,
\end{align}
which yields
\begin{align}
    x_{\mathcal{B}}^{t+1}
    =
    x_{\mathcal{B}}^{t}
    -
    \underbrace{x_q^{t+1}}_{\in \mathbb{R}} \cdot \overbrace{\underbrace{A_{:\mathcal{B}}^{-1} A_{:q}}_{:=d}}^{\text{requires solving a linear system}}
    \enspace.
\end{align}

Question: how to chose $x_q^{t+1}$ then?
Simply by chosing $x_q^{t+1}$ it as large possible, but still satisfy the constraint $x^{t+1} \geq 0$:

<div class="border">
  <p>
  \begin{align}
    x_q^{t+1} = \min_{i \in \mathcal{B} | d_i > 0} \frac{x_i}{d_i}
    \enspace,
  \end{align}
  </p>
</div>

with $d:= A_{:\mathcal{B}}^{-1} A_{:q}$ ($d$ stands for direction of the update).

\begin{algorithm}
\caption{(Revised) Simplex}
\begin{algorithmic}
\Procedure{Simplex}{$A, c, x_0$}
  \For{$i \in 0, \dots, n_{\mathrm{iter}} $}
      \State $\lambda = (A_{:\mathcal{B}}^\top)^{-1} c_{\mathcal{B}}$
      \State $\mu_{\mathcal{B}^c} = c_{\mathcal{B}^c} - A_{\mathcal{B}^c}^\top (A_{:\mathcal{B}}^\top)^{-1} c_{\mathcal{B}} $
      \IF{$\mu_j \geq 0$}
        \State Return $x^{t}$
      \ENDIF
      \State $q  \in \arg \min_{j \in \mathcal{B}^c}  \, \mu_{j} $
      \State $d = A_{:\mathcal{B}}^{-1} A_{:q}$
      \State $ x_q^{t+1} = \min_{i \in \mathcal{B} | d_i > 0} \frac{x_i}{d_i} $
      \State $x_{\mathcal{B}}^{t+1}
              = x_{\mathcal{B}}^{t} - x_q^{t+1} \cdot d$
  \EndFor
  \Return x
\EndProcedure
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}
